# Cognisec AI: Viva Presentation Speaker Notes

**Project Title:** Cognisec AI: Network Behaviour Fingerprinting for Unseen Attack Detection — An Experimental Comparison of XGBoost and Variational Autoencoders  
**Target Audience:** Academic Examination Committee / Final Year Project Viva Panel  
**Presentation Time:** ~15–20 minutes  
**Presenter:** Santhoskrishna  

---

### Slide 1: Title and Research Problem
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "Good morning, respected committee members and professors. Today, I am presenting my research project titled **Cognisec AI: Network Behaviour Fingerprinting for Unseen Attack Detection — An Experimental Comparison of XGBoost and Variational Autoencoders**.  
  In modern network security, intrusion detection systems face an existential challenge: supervised machine learning models achieve near-perfect accuracy on known attacks, but their closed-world assumption causes them to fail catastrophically when encountering unseen, zero-day attacks. This project investigates whether unsupervised latent density modelling—specifically a Tabular Variational Autoencoder trained exclusively on benign traffic—can reliably detect unseen attacks, and how its operational trade-offs compare against state-of-the-art supervised tree boosting with XGBoost."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Clearly state that the project compares two fundamentally different learning paradigms: supervised discrimination versus benign-only normal-behaviour manifold learning.

---

### Slide 2: Motivation and Research Gap
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "Traditional signature-based and supervised intrusion detection systems require prior exposure to attack patterns. In academic literature, models frequently report over 99% accuracy because they are evaluated on random test splits where every attack family was already present in the training set.  
  In the real world, this assumption is invalid. Attackers constantly mutate payloads, bypass known signatures, and deploy zero-day exploits. The research gap we address is: **How do supervised and unsupervised models behave when evaluated under a strict, leakage-controlled protocol where entire attack families are held out during training?**"
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Emphasize that high reported accuracy in existing literature is often an artifact of poor experimental design and train-test contamination.

---

### Slide 3: Research Objectives and Core Questions
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "To investigate this gap, we established three primary research objectives:  
  First, to formulate a reproducible, leakage-controlled data pipeline on authentic CICIDS2017 network flow records, strictly isolating identity features from behavioral features.  
  Second, to benchmark supervised XGBoost against a PyTorch Tabular VAE across both known attacks (Experiment A) and held-out attack families (Experiment B).  
  Third, to rigorously analyze the operational trade-offs—including inference latency, false-alarm budget constraints, statistical significance via paired bootstrap and McNemar tests, and distribution shift sensitivity."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Point out that the research does not assume one model is 'better', but rather measures their precise mathematical and operational trade-offs.

---

### Slide 4: CICIDS2017 Benchmark Dataset and Provenance
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "We selected the authentic CICIDS2017 dataset, published by Sharafaldin, Lashkari, and Ghorbani at the Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity, UNB.  
  The dataset encompasses five days of realistic network activity generated over an attack-and-victim testbed. It comprises eight raw CSV files generated by CICFlowMeter, covering over 2.8 million raw flow records across twelve distinct threat categories, including DoS, DDoS, PortScan, Brute Force, Web Attacks, Botnets, and Infiltration."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Cite the official Sharafaldin et al. (2018) ICISSP publication. Emphasize that all eight original raw CSVs are preserved and hash-verified in the project repository.

---

### Slide 5: Data Cleaning, Preprocessing, and Leakage Prevention
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "A common failure in ML-based security papers is socket leakage. If source IPs, destination IPs, ports, or timestamps remain in the feature set, the model simply memorizes the IP address of the attacking machine rather than learning network behavior.  
  In our pipeline, all eight socket-identity features were stripped. Division-by-zero infinities and missing values were systematically handled. Exactly seventy behavioral flow features were retained, capturing temporal dynamics, packet size distributions, and TCP flag patterns.  
  Crucially, all scaling parameters were fitted strictly on the training partition and saved in our preprocessor artifact, eliminating test-set contamination."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Confirm that exactly 70 behavioral features are retained, and that socket identity leakage was completely prevented.

---

### Slide 6: Experimental Design and Held-Out Attack Protocol
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "Our experimental design establishes two primary benchmarks:  
  In **Experiment A (Known Attacks)**, models are evaluated on test traffic containing attack families represented during training: DoS, PortScan, and Brute Force, totaling 85,088 attack flows alongside 40,000 benign flows.  
  In **Experiment B (Held-Out Attacks)**, we evaluate on four threat families that were completely excluded from model training and validation: DDoS, Web Attacks, Botnets, and Infiltration, totaling 132,209 attack flows evaluated against the exact same 40,000 benign baseline flows.  
  This design allows us to isolate the exact performance penalty incurred when encountering unseen threat families."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Explain the record count reconciliation: 257,297 unique physical test records ($40,000 + 85,088 + 132,209$) yielding 297,297 total benchmark evaluation decisions across Experiments A and B.

---

### Slide 7: Supervised XGBoost: Architecture and Optimization
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "For the supervised baseline, we implemented Extreme Gradient Boosting with 200 histogram-based estimators and a maximum tree depth of 6.  
  XGBoost minimizes the binary logistic loss with early stopping based on validation log-loss. Probability predictions are obtained through the logistic sigmoid of leaf log-odds.  
  Using validation grid search, we calibrated the decision threshold at 0.52 to maximize the F1-score while maintaining sub-millisecond inference throughput."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  XGBoost is a discriminative boundary learner; its parameters were tuned on validation data with early stopping to prevent overfitting.

---

### Slide 8: Tabular PyTorch VAE: Architecture and Benign-Only Training
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "For unsupervised anomaly detection, we built a Tabular PyTorch Variational Autoencoder.  
  The encoder compresses the 70-dimensional input through dense hidden layers of 64 and 32 neurons with LeakyReLU and batch normalization, mapping into a 16-dimensional latent Gaussian space parameterized by mean vector mu and log-variance sigma squared.  
  The decoder mirrors this architecture to reconstruct the original features.  
  Critically, the VAE is trained exclusively on benign traffic using the beta-ELBO loss with beta set to 0.005. It learns the normal manifold of legitimate network operations without ever seeing an attack vector."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Explain the reparameterization trick ($z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$) and why $\beta = 0.005$ was chosen to avoid posterior collapse on tabular data.

---

### Slide 9: Anomaly Scoring and False-Alarm Budget Calibration
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "For the VAE, inference is based on Mean Squared Reconstruction Error across the 70 behavioral features. When benign flows traverse the autoencoder, their reconstruction error is low. When an anomalous flow violates the learned correlations, its reconstruction error spikes.  
  In operational security, false alarms overwhelm security analysts. Therefore, we calibrated the VAE decision threshold strictly on the benign validation partition at the 99th percentile, setting tau to 0.330610. This mathematically caps the baseline false-positive rate at 1.0%."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Emphasize that the threshold was selected on validation data, not tuned on the test set.

---

### Slide 10: Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Methodology
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "Given severe class imbalance in intrusion detection, standard accuracy is misleading. We focus primarily on Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC), detection Recall, and False Positive Rate.  
  Furthermore, to avoid unsubstantiated claims, we performed formal inferential testing. Because both models evaluate the exact same flow instances, we applied McNemar's paired chi-squared test for binary decisions and paired non-parametric bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations to derive 95% confidence intervals for metric deltas. We report statistically sound bounds (p < 0.001) rather than rounded zeros."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Explain why McNemar's test is appropriate for paired binary outcomes and why paired bootstrap correctly handles sample dependence.

---

### Slide 11: Experiment A: Known Attack Benchmark Results
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "On known attacks, supervised XGBoost demonstrates near-flawless performance: 99.99% recall, 99.95% precision, PR-AUC of 0.9999, and an FPR of just 0.0975%.  
  The VAE, operating under its 1.0% false-alarm budget, achieves 25.89% recall with 98.34% precision and PR-AUC of 0.9432.  
  The performance delta in F1-score is +0.5898 in favor of XGBoost (95% CI: 0.5859 to 0.5934, p < 0.001, McNemar chi-squared 63,305). When attack patterns are known, supervised boosting is undeniably superior."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Acknowledge XGBoost's decisive victory on known signatures while noting VAE's high precision even with lower recall.

---

### Slide 12: Experiment B: Held-Out Attack Family Results
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "When confronted with unseen attack families in Experiment B, both models degrade, but in revealing ways.  
  XGBoost's overall recall drops to 62.50%, with PR-AUC at 0.9924. The VAE achieves 16.60% recall with 98.33% precision.  
  However, examining aggregate numbers alone obscures critical vulnerabilities, which brings us to our per-family breakdown."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Highlight that aggregate recall on held-out attacks is heavily influenced by the high volume of DDoS flows.

---

### Slide 13: Per-Family Disaggregation and the Infiltration Finding
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "When we break down Experiment B by threat family, the fundamental limitation of supervised learning is laid bare:  
  On **DDoS** (128,027 flows), XGBoost achieves 63.01% recall because volumetric DDoS closely resembles the DoS Hulk traffic present in training.  
  However, on **Infiltration** (36 stealth flows executing privilege escalation via Metasploit), XGBoost achieved **zero percent recall**—failing to detect a single flow!  
  In stark contrast, the Tabular VAE successfully detected **47.22% of Infiltration flows**. Because Infiltration subtly alters latent feature correlations without generating volumetric spikes, the VAE's benign manifold caught the anomaly that completely slipped past XGBoost's discriminative boundary."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  This is the crown jewel of the research: proving empirical complementarity where an unsupervised model detects stealthy attacks missed entirely by a supervised model.

---

### Slide 14: Complementarity Analysis and False-Alarm Trade-Offs
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "Analyzing mutual detection across all 132,209 held-out attacks:  
  Both models detected 21,521 flows.  
  XGBoost uniquely detected 61,114 flows (overwhelmingly DDoS).  
  The VAE uniquely detected 420 flows (including the stealth infiltration vectors).  
  Both models missed 49,154 flows.  
  Furthermore, in our threshold sensitivity analysis (Experiment C), relaxing the VAE's false-alarm budget from 1% to 10% increases recall to 93.66%, illustrating the inescapable operational trade-off between sensitivity and false alarm fatigue."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  The 420 VAE-unique detections demonstrate that the VAE provides orthogonal security value, not just a weaker version of XGBoost.

### Slide 17: Research Limitations and Threats to Validity
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "Academic integrity requires transparently documenting our limitations:  
  First, CICIDS2017 was collected in 2017; its synthetic background traffic and specific attack tools may not reflect modern encrypted enterprise networks.  
  Second, holding out a family within a benchmark is a controlled proxy, not identical to encountering a truly novel real-world zero-day exploit.  
  Third, the sample size for Infiltration is small (36 flows), which limits statistical power for that specific class.  
  Finally, this benchmark operates on pre-extracted flow summaries; production deployment would require real-time streaming feature extraction engines."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Acknowledging limitations demonstrates scientific maturity and deep understanding of network security realities.

---

### Slide 18: Conclusions and Future Research
- **Spoken Script:**  
  "In conclusion, our research demonstrates that:  
  Supervised models like XGBoost are optimal for high-throughput, frontline defense against known attack patterns due to sub-microsecond latency and negligible false alarms.  
  However, unsupervised generative models like our Tabular VAE provide indispensable orthogonal coverage for stealthy, structurally novel intrusions.  
  The optimal future direction for NIDS is not choosing between supervised and unsupervised learning, but rather deploying a **hybrid, multi-tiered architecture**: using XGBoost for rapid frontline filtering and escalating borderline or low-confidence flows to a generative autoencoder for zero-day anomaly triage."
- **Key Defense Point:**  
  Deliver a confident, forward-looking synthesis of the research findings.

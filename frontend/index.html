Supervised Model: Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost) Supervised Discriminator

Trained on both benign traffic and represented attack signatures (DoS, PortScan, Brute Force). Optimized for sharp classification boundaries and extremely low false alarms.

Anomaly Model: Tabular Variational Autoencoder (VAE) Benign-Only Manifold

Trained strictly on normal benign network flows without ever observing any attack patterns. Flags anomalies based on high latent reconstruction divergence (Mean Squared Error).

  • Normalcy Modelling: Learns Gaussian latent distribution ($\mu, \sigma^2$) of benign flows.
  • Stealth Complementarity: Successfully detects 47.22% of infiltration flows missed by XGBoost.
  • Operational Trade-off: Lower precision under realistic 1.0% false-alarm budget constraints.
  • Calibrated Threshold: 0.3306 (calibrated at 1.0% validation FPR ceiling).
Key Research Findings & Empirical Summary Peer-Audited Results

1. The Known Attack Illusion

While XGBoost scores 99.99% PR-AUC on known attack families, a data integrity audit revealed that 14,682 identical feature vectors crossed the train/test boundary due to automated repetitive probe packets.

2. Partial Unseen Transfer

XGBoost detected 63.01% of held-out DDoS traffic because volumetric attack mechanics closely mirrored trained DoS Hulk flows, not because of generalized zero-day awareness.

3. Targeted Complementarity

For subtle, low-rate infiltration flows, supervised decision trees failed completely (0.0% recall), whereas the VAE detected 47.22%, confirming that anomaly models serve as specialized tripwires.

Key Scientific Takeaway: The Stealth Infiltration Paradox

Why does XGBoost achieve 62.5% overall held-out recall while still failing on novel attacks?

The Infiltration Vulnerability

Infiltration flows (36 total flows in CICIDS2017) execute stealthy remote command execution and privilege escalation with normal packet rates and typical TCP window sizes. Supervised XGBoost classifies 100% of these flows as Benign (0.0% recall) because no identical signature was present in training.

Conversely, the VAE detected 47.22% of Infiltration instances, proving that semi-supervised latent models provide indispensable complementarity as tripwires for stealthy attacks.

Sample Population Warning: Infiltration constitutes only 36 flows in the CICIDS2017 test set. While the 47.22% recall is statistically meaningful, caution is advised when extrapolating to enterprise production scale.

Research Methodology & Integrity Audit

End-to-end scientific workflow, leakage prevention safeguards, and candid audit of real-world benchmark limitations.

Critical Research Audit: 14,682 Cross-Partition Duplicate Vectors

A rigorous feature audit revealed that 14,682 identical feature vectors cross the training and testing boundaries within represented attack classes (2,266 DoS, 1,261 PortScan, 159 Brute Force, and 146 Benign). These occur due to automated, repetitive network probe packets (e.g. zero-payload TCP SYN sweeps).

Scientific Implication: The near-perfect 99.99% PR-AUC achieved by XGBoost on known attacks is partially inflated by decision trees memorizing repeated feature vectors. However, exactly ZERO identical flows crossed partition boundaries for held-out zero-day families (DDoS, Web Attack, Bot, Infiltration), preserving the scientific validity and rigor of the unseen evaluation.
